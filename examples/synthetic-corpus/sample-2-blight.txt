The Blight Wasn't the Blight

Half the forum threads I read this spring blamed late blight the moment a tomato leaf
yellowed, and I believed them for about two weeks, pulling and bagging anything with a spot
on it; the leaf drop kept spreading anyway, which a real blight infection doesn't usually do
once the infected material is gone. Turns out it was early blight the whole time, a slower,
duller fungus that doesn't kill the plant outright, it just keeps knocking leaves down from
the bottom up until the plant is standing on a bare stalk with fruit still ripening at the
top; the two diseases get lumped together by name and by panic, but they behave, spread, and
respond to treatment completely differently.

Once I had the right name for it, the fix was unglamorous: strip the lower third of each
plant early, before the fungus gets there, and keep the lowest leaves off wet soil entirely.

Some notes on what I'm not claiming. I'm not saying late blight can't happen here; a
neighbor two streets over lost a whole row to it three years ago, and I'm not doubting that.
I'm saying that a yellow leaf and a bit of forum panic are not a diagnosis, and that the two
diseases are worth telling apart before you pull a plant that only has the milder one.

The bare-stalk-with-fruit-on-top look is ugly enough that I understand the instinct to yank
the whole plant. Don't. The fruit up top doesn't know the bottom third is gone.
