A Schedule Isn't the Same as Enough

For three summers I watered on a schedule, every other morning, twenty minutes per bed,
because that was the number a nursery employee gave me once and I never questioned it
again, and the beds mostly survived, which I took as proof the number was right; survival
and health are not the same measurement, though, and I only learned that when a hot week
pushed the schedule past what the soil could actually absorb before the next round arrived.
Turns out the right question was never how often, it was how deep, and a moisture reading
six inches down told me things the calendar never could.

So the beds are off the calendar now. They get water when the six-inch reading says to, not
before, and that has meant some weeks with no watering at all and others with water every
single day during a real heat spike; the schedule was a proxy for the thing I actually
needed to know, and a mediocre proxy at that.

I'll say plainly what I haven't verified: I don't know whether this holds for container
gardens the same way, since containers dry from more directions at once, and I haven't run
the six-inch test on a single pot yet. For beds in open ground, though, I'd trade the
calendar for the probe every time.

The nursery employee wasn't wrong to give me a number to start with. Everyone needs a first
guess. It just isn't the last one.
